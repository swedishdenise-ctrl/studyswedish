Introduction

Välkommen

Welcome to 50 Svenska Ordspråk

Ordspråk — proverbs — are windows into how a culture thinks. Swedish proverbs are especially revealing: you'll find an obsession with moderation (lagom), a deep love of nature, a calm fatalism about outcomes, and a dry humor that surfaces most clearly in the literal translations.

Each entry gives you four things: the Swedish proverb in full, a phonetic pronunciation guide, the word-for-word literal translation (often surprisingly funny), and the actual meaning in English.

The literal translations are the heart of this collection. When Swedes say "there's no cow on the ice," they mean "don't worry." When they say "hurry slowly," they mean slow down to avoid mistakes. Seeing the gap between the literal and the intended is one of the best ways to feel a language from the inside.

If you have the audio companion, listen along as I read each proverb aloud — twice, clearly, so you can absorb the rhythm and sounds of real Swedish speech.

Pronunciation Key

All phonetic guides use English sounds. ALL-CAPS letters carry the main stress.

å "AW" as in saw
ä "EH" / "AIR" as in bed
ö "UR" as in fern
j "Y" as in yes
g + e/i/y/ä/ö "Y" as in yes
sk/k + e/i/y/ä/ö "SH" as in she

Tip: Read each proverb aloud. Swedish has a musical pitch accent — some words rise, others fall. The best way to learn it is simply to listen and imitate.

The 50 Proverbs

01 – 50
1
Lagom är bäst
LAH-gom air BEST
Literal"The right amount is best"
MeaningEverything in moderation — not too much, not too little.
The word lagom is uniquely Swedish and essentially untranslatable: exactly the right amount, no more, no less. It is arguably the single most important concept in Swedish culture.
2
Det är ingen ko på isen
det air ING-en KOO paw EE-sen
Literal"There's no cow on the ice"
MeaningThere's no need to worry — everything is under control.
Picture a cow that has wandered onto a frozen lake. That would be a crisis. If there's no cow on the ice, there is no crisis.
3
Kärt barn har många namn
CHÄRT BAHN hahr MONG-ah NAHM
Literal"A dear child has many names"
MeaningSomething beloved gets lots of names and nicknames.
Used to explain why something — a person, a place, a dish — has many different words for it. Affection breeds creativity.
4
Man ska inte sälja skinnet innan björnen är skjuten
man SKAH IN-teh SELL-yah SHIN-et IN-an BYUR-nen air SHU-ten
Literal"Don't sell the hide before the bear is shot"
MeaningDon't count your chickens before they hatch.
A hunter who sells the bearskin before the hunt is over is going to be in trouble. Assume success only once you have it.
5
Den som gapar efter mycket mister ofta hela stycket
den som GAH-par EF-ter MYK-et MIS-ter OF-tah HEE-lah STYK-et
Literal"He who gapes for much often loses the whole piece"
MeaningGreed leads to loss — reaching for everything can leave you with nothing.
6
Bättre sent än aldrig
BET-reh SENT en AL-drig
Literal"Better late than never"
MeaningDoing something late is still better than never doing it at all.
7
Morgonstund har guld i mund
MOR-gon-stund hahr GULD ee MUND
Literal"Morning time has gold in its mouth"
MeaningThe early bird catches the worm — early hours are precious.
Mund is an archaic form of mun (mouth), giving the proverb a poetic, old-fashioned feel even in modern Swedish.
8
Ingen rök utan eld
ING-en RURK OO-tan ELD
Literal"No smoke without fire"
MeaningWhere there's a rumor, there's usually some truth to it.
9
Liten tuva stjälper stort lass
LEE-ten TU-vah SHEL-per STOORT LASS
Literal"A small tuft of grass overturns a big load"
MeaningSmall things can have big consequences — a seemingly minor obstacle can ruin everything.
The image is a heavy cart tipping over when its wheel catches a small grassy mound on the road. The Swedish version of "the straw that broke the camel's back."
10
Borta bra men hemma bäst
BOR-tah BRAH men HEM-ah BEST
Literal"Away is good but home is best"
MeaningThere's no place like home.
Said with a contented sigh upon returning from a trip, however wonderful it was.
11
Det är inte guld allt som glimmar
det air IN-teh GULD alt som GLIM-ar
Literal"Not everything that glitters is gold"
MeaningAppearances can be deceiving.
12
Bra karl reder sig själv
BRAH KARL REH-der sig SHELV
Literal"A good man manages himself"
MeaningA capable person is self-reliant and doesn't need help.
Can be used both admiringly (resourceful!) and critically (too proud to ask for help). Very Swedish in its ambivalence about dependence.
13
Många bäckar små gör en stor å
MONG-ah BEK-ar SMOH YURR en STOOR OH
Literal"Many small streams make a big river"
MeaningMany small contributions add up to something big — every little bit helps.
Note: å means both the vowel and "river/stream." A delightful double meaning the original speaker almost certainly intended.
14
Hellre fågeln i handen än tio i skogen
HEL-reh FOW-geln ee HAN-den en TEE-oh ee SKOO-gen
Literal"Better the bird in the hand than ten in the forest"
MeaningA bird in the hand is worth two in the bush — take the certain good over the uncertain greater good.
15
Övning ger färdighet
UV-ning YAIR FAIR-dig-het
Literal"Practice gives skill"
MeaningPractice makes perfect.
Encouragement often given to language learners — including those learning Swedish.
16
Den som väntar på något gott väntar aldrig för länge
den som VEN-tar paw NAW-got GOT VEN-tar AL-drig FUR LENG-eh
Literal"He who waits for something good never waits too long"
MeaningGood things come to those who wait.
17
Skratt förlänger livet
SKRATT FUR-leng-er LEE-vet
Literal"Laughter lengthens life"
MeaningLaughter is the best medicine.
18
Som man bäddar får man ligga
som man BED-ar for man LIG-ah
Literal"As you make your bed, so you must lie in it"
MeaningYou have to live with the consequences of your choices.
19
Äpplet faller inte långt från trädet
EP-let FAL-er IN-teh LONGT fron TRED-et
Literal"The apple doesn't fall far from the tree"
MeaningChildren tend to resemble and repeat their parents' patterns.
20
En svala gör ingen sommar
en SVAH-lah YURR ING-en SOM-ar
Literal"One swallow doesn't make a summer"
MeaningOne positive sign doesn't mean the hard times are over — one instance doesn't prove a pattern.
In Sweden, the first swallow of the season is eagerly awaited. But seeing just one doesn't mean summer has arrived.
21
Tyst vatten har djupt botten
TYST VAT-en hahr YUPT BOT-en
Literal"Still water has a deep bottom"
MeaningStill waters run deep — quiet people often have more depth than they show.
Particularly apt in Sweden, where reserve and understatement are cultural norms. Silence often signals depth, not emptiness.
22
Ord är silver, tystnad är guld
OORD air SIL-ver TYST-nad air GULD
Literal"Words are silver, silence is gold"
MeaningSpeech is silver, silence is golden — sometimes saying nothing is the wisest choice.
23
Gammal kärlek rostar inte
GAM-al CHAIR-lek ROOST-ar IN-teh
Literal"Old love doesn't rust"
MeaningAn old flame never fully dies — deep feelings resurface even after years apart.
24
Tid läker alla sår
TEED LEH-ker AL-ah SOHR
Literal"Time heals all wounds"
MeaningWith enough time, even the deepest pain and grief will fade.
25
Man talar om varg och vargen kommer
man TAH-lar om VARG ok VAR-gen KOM-er
Literal"Speak of the wolf and the wolf appears"
MeaningSpeak of the devil — said when the person you're talking about suddenly appears.
Sweden's version uses a wolf instead of the devil. Fitting for a country with real wolves in the forest.
26
Skynda långsamt
SHYN-dah LONG-samt
Literal"Hurry slowly"
MeaningMore haste, less speed — slow down to avoid the mistakes that come from rushing.
From the Latin festina lente. Two words that seem to cancel each other out, and yet make perfect sense together.
27
Allas ansvar är ingens ansvar
AL-as ANS-var air ING-ens ANS-var
Literal"Everyone's responsibility is no one's responsibility"
MeaningWhen a task belongs to everyone, no one steps up to do it.
The Swedish version of the "tragedy of the commons." Particularly ironic in a society famous for collective responsibility.
28
Som man sår, får man skörda
som man SOHR for man SHURR-dah
Literal"As you sow, so shall you reap"
MeaningYour actions have consequences — what you put in is what you get out.
29
Var sak har sin tid
vahr SAHK hahr sin TEED
Literal"Every thing has its time"
MeaningEverything happens in its own time — there is a right moment for everything.
30
Kärleken är blind
CHAIR-lek-en air BLIND
Literal"Love is blind"
MeaningWhen you're in love, you cannot see flaws — in your beloved or yourself.
31
Ingen är profet i sitt eget land
ING-en air pro-FEET ee sit EE-get LAND
Literal"No one is a prophet in their own land"
MeaningIt's hard to be respected where you grew up — familiarity can breed contempt.
From the Gospel of Luke. A warning against expecting recognition from those closest to home.
32
Kärlek och hosta kan inte döljas
CHAIR-lek ok HOOS-tah kan IN-teh DUL-yas
Literal"Love and a cough cannot be hidden"
MeaningSome things are impossible to conceal, no matter how hard you try.
One of the most charming proverbs in this collection — the pairing of love and a cough is unexpectedly perfect.
33
Varje moln har en silverkant
VAR-yeh MOLN hahr en SIL-ver-kant
Literal"Every cloud has a silver lining"
MeaningThere is something positive to be found in every negative situation.
34
Lycklig i spel, olycklig i kärlek
LYK-lig ee SPEEL oh-LYK-lig ee CHAIR-lek
Literal"Lucky in games, unlucky in love"
MeaningYou can't be lucky at everything — fortune balances itself out.
Said with a sympathetic smile when someone wins at cards but can't seem to make a relationship work.
35
Det ordnar sig
det ORD-nar sig
Literal"It will sort itself out"
MeaningThings will work out in the end — don't worry too much about it.
Perhaps the most Swedish phrase in this entire collection. Three words that capture a deep national confidence in the eventual rightness of things.
36
Det är lättare sagt än gjort
det air LET-areh SAGT en YOORT
Literal"It is easier said than done"
MeaningTaking action is always harder than talking about it.
37
Den som sover syndar inte
den som SOO-ver SYN-dar IN-teh
Literal"He who sleeps does not sin"
MeaningYou can't cause trouble while you're asleep — sometimes doing nothing is the wisest course.
Often said with a smile when justifying a long lie-in. Sweden takes its sleep seriously.
38
Hjälp dig själv, så hjälper Gud dig
YELP dig SHELV soh YEL-per GUUD dig
Literal"Help yourself and God will help you"
MeaningGod helps those who help themselves — take initiative before expecting outside help.
39
Inga nyheter, goda nyheter
ING-ah NY-heh-ter GOO-dah NY-heh-ter
Literal"No news, good news"
MeaningIf you haven't heard anything bad, things are probably fine.
40
Det händer den bäste
det HEN-der den BES-teh
Literal"It happens to the best"
MeaningEven the most capable people make mistakes — it can happen to anyone.
Generous consolation, offered immediately after someone drops something, forgets a meeting, or makes a silly error.
41
Man kan inte ha allt
man kan IN-teh hah ALT
Literal"You can't have everything"
MeaningWe all have to accept limitations — demanding everything is unrealistic.
42
Pengar luktar inte
PENG-ar LUK-tar IN-teh
Literal"Money has no smell"
MeaningMoney is money, regardless of how it was earned — the source doesn't change its value.
From the Latin pecunia non olet — attributed to Roman Emperor Vespasian, who taxed public urinals and used the profits to fund Rome.
43
Att göra en höna av en fjäder
at YURR-ah en HU-nah ahv en FYEH-der
Literal"To make a hen out of a feather"
MeaningTo make a mountain out of a molehill — to greatly exaggerate a small problem.
One of the most vivid expressions in Swedish. You started with a single feather and somehow ended up with a whole hen.
44
Ju fler kockar, desto sämre soppa
yu FLAIR KOK-ar DES-toh SEM-reh SOP-ah
Literal"The more cooks, the worse the soup"
MeaningToo many cooks spoil the broth — too many people involved in a task leads to a worse result.
45
Det lönar sig att vara ärlig
det LU-nar sig at VAH-rah AIR-lig
Literal"It pays to be honest"
MeaningHonesty is the best policy — truth-telling ultimately rewards you.
46
Man ska inte kasta pärlor för svin
man SKAH IN-teh KAS-tah PAIR-lor FUR SVEEN
Literal"Don't cast pearls before swine"
MeaningDon't offer something valuable to those who won't appreciate it.
From the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 7:6). Same meaning in Swedish as in English.
47
Ensam är stark
EN-sam air STARK
Literal"Alone is strong"
MeaningIndependence and self-reliance are strengths, not weaknesses.
A distinctly Swedish proverb reflecting the national value placed on individual independence. Not lonely — strong.
48
Vågat är halvt vunnet
VOH-gat air HALVT VUN-et
Literal"Dared is half won"
MeaningNothing ventured, nothing gained — having the courage to start is already half the battle.
Excellent encouragement for anyone about to take a bold step — apply for the job, send the message, book the trip.
49
Den som gräver en grop för andra faller själv däri
den som GREV-er en GROOP foor AN-drah FAL-er SHELV deh-REE
Literal"He who digs a pit for others falls in it himself"
MeaningSchemes intended to harm others tend to backfire on the schemer.
From the Book of Proverbs (26:27). Karma, expressed as a cautionary tale about digging.
50
Livet är kort, konsten lång
LEE-vet air KOORT KONS-ten LONG
Literal"Life is short, art is long"
MeaningThere is always more to learn and create than a lifetime can hold — keep going.
From the Latin ars longa, vita brevis. A beautiful final proverb for a collection about language — the most human art of all.
