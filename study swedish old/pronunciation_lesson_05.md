# Pronunciation Lesson 5: J, DJ, LJ, HJ — The Swedish Y-Sounds

## What You'll Learn
Swedish has several letter combinations that all produce the same sound: the "y" sound (like English "yes"). Once you know them, you'll stop mispronouncing some of the most common words in the language — starting with "jag" (I/me), which is probably the first word you'll ever say in Swedish.

---

## The Core Rule

**The letter J in Swedish always sounds like the English "y" in "yes."**

Never like the English J in "jam," "jump," or "juice." Never. Not even once. Swedish J is always soft, always "y."

This means:
- **jag** = "yah" — NOT "jag"
- **ja** = "yah" — NOT "jah" with a hard J
- **juice** = "yoos" — NOT "jooss"
- **jobb** = "yob" — NOT "job"

If you remember nothing else from this lesson, remember this: **J = Y. Always.**

---

## J — The Most Common Y-Sound

J is everywhere in Swedish. It appears in some of the most frequently used words in the language.

### Everyday J Words

- **jag** /yah/ — I, me (the most common word in Swedish — and the G is silent!)
- **ja** /yah/ — yes
- **jobb** /yob/ — job, work
- **jobba** /YOB-ah/ — to work
- **ju** /yoo/ — you know, indeed (emphasis word)
- **just** /yoost/ — exactly, just
- **jätte-** /YEH-teh/ — very, really (prefix: jättebra = really good)
- **jul** /yool/ — Christmas
- **juni** /YOO-nee/ — June
- **juli** /YOO-lee/ — July

### J in the Middle and End of Words

J keeps its "y" sound wherever it appears in a word:

- **fjäril** /FYEH-ril/ — butterfly
- **fjäll** /fyehl/ — mountain (alpine)
- **mjölk** /myuhlk/ — milk
- **mjuk** /myook/ — soft
- **njuta** /NYOO-tah/ — to enjoy
- **hjälp** /yelp/ — help (the H is silent — more on this below)

---

## DJ — Same Sound, Silent D

When you see DJ at the beginning of a word, the D is completely silent. Only the J sound ("y") is pronounced.

- **djur** /yoor/ — animal
- **djup** /yoop/ — deep
- **djärv** /yehrv/ — bold, daring
- **djungel** /YOONG-el/ — jungle
- **djävul** /YEH-vool/ — devil

**"Djur" is just "yoor."** The D adds nothing to the pronunciation. It's purely historical — Swedish kept the old spelling even though the D stopped being pronounced centuries ago.

---

## LJ — Same Sound, Silent L

When LJ appears at the beginning of a word, the L is silent. Only the J sound ("y") is pronounced.

- **ljus** /yoos/ — light, candle, bright
- **ljud** /yood/ — sound
- **ljuga** /YOO-gah/ — to lie (tell a lie)
- **ljum** /yoom/ — lukewarm
- **ljuv** /yoov/ — lovely, sweet

**"Ljus" (light/candle) is just "yoos."** No L sound at all. This is one of those words that looks impossible to pronounce but is actually just two sounds: y + oos.

### LJ in the Middle of Words

When LJ appears in the middle of a word, both letters can sometimes be slightly heard, but it still leans heavily toward a "y" sound:

- **hälja** — the L is very soft, almost gone
- **valje** — similar treatment

In practice, most Swedes just say "y" for LJ regardless of position. You can safely do the same.

---

## HJ — Same Sound, Silent H

When HJ appears at the beginning of a word, the H is silent. Only the J sound ("y") is pronounced.

- **hjälp** /yelp/ — help
- **hjälte** /YEL-teh/ — hero
- **hjärta** /YEHR-tah/ — heart
- **hjärna** /YEHR-nah/ — brain
- **hjul** /yool/ — wheel
- **hjort** /yort/ — deer

**"Hjälp" (help) sounds exactly like the English word "yelp."** And "hjärta" (heart) is "yehr-tah." The H is completely invisible to pronunciation.

This is another case where Swedish preserved old spellings. Hundreds of years ago, the H was pronounced. Today it exists only in writing.

---

## GJ — Same Sound, Silent G

You briefly met this in Lesson 4, but it belongs here too. When GJ appears at the beginning of a word, the G is silent. Only the "y" sound remains.

- **gjort** /yort/ — done (past participle of göra)
- **gjuta** /YOO-tah/ — to cast, to pour
- **gjorde** /YOR-deh/ — did (past tense of göra)

**"Gjorde" (did) is "yor-deh."** Not "gyor-deh." The G vanishes completely.

---

## The Complete Picture

Here's every letter combination that makes the "y" sound:

| Spelling | Silent Letter | Example | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|----------|--------------|---------|--------------|---------|
| **J** | — | jag | /yah/ | I, me |
| **DJ** | D is silent | djur | /yoor/ | animal |
| **LJ** | L is silent | ljus | /yoos/ | light |
| **HJ** | H is silent | hjälp | /yelp/ | help |
| **GJ** | G is silent | gjort | /yort/ | done |
| **G** (before soft vowel) | — | göra | /YUH-rah/ | to do |

Six different ways to write the same sound. Welcome to Swedish spelling.

---

## Why So Many Spellings?

A reasonable question. The answer is history. Centuries ago, these consonant clusters were actually pronounced — Swedes really did say the D in "djur," the L in "ljus," and the H in "hjälp." Over time, the pronunciation simplified but the spelling stayed the same.

Think of it like English "knight" — the K and GH were once pronounced. Swedish did the same thing, just with different letters.

The good news: once you know the pattern, it's completely predictable. **DJ, LJ, HJ, GJ at the start of a word = just say "y."**

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Mistake 1: Pronouncing J like English J**
Wrong: "jag" as "jag" (like "jag-uar")
Right: "jag" as "yah"

**Mistake 2: Pronouncing the silent letters**
Wrong: "djur" as "d-yoor"
Right: "djur" as "yoor"

Wrong: "ljus" as "l-yoos"
Right: "ljus" as "yoos"

Wrong: "hjälp" as "h-yelp"
Right: "hjälp" as "yelp"

**Mistake 3: Mixing up J-sound and SJ-sound**
The J-sound (this lesson) = "y" as in "yes" — light, front of mouth
The SJ-sound (Lesson 3) = "hwuh" — heavy, back of throat

These are completely different sounds. Don't confuse them:
- **jö** = "yuh" (J-sound)
- **sjö** = "hwuh" (SJ-sound, meaning "lake")

## Practice Exercise

Read these sentences out loud. Every bolded letter or combination should be pronounced as "y":

**1. Jag hjälper dig gärna.**
/yah YEL-per dey YAIR-nah/
"I'll gladly help you."
*(**J**ag = y. **Hj**älper = y, H silent. **G**ärna = y, soft G.)*

**2. Det var ljust och ljuvligt i juni.**
/deh var yoost ohk YOOV-lit ee YOO-nee/
"It was bright and lovely in June."
*(**Lj**ust = y, L silent. **Lj**uvligt = y, L silent. **J**uni = y.)*

**3. Djuren gjorde ljud hela natten.**
/YOO-ren YOR-deh yood HEH-lah NAH-ten/
"The animals made sounds all night."
*(**Dj**uren = y, D silent. **Gj**orde = y, G silent. **Lj**ud = y, L silent.)*

**4. Ge mig juice, jag jobbar med hjärtat.**
/yeh mey yoos, yah YOB-ar med YEHR-tat/
"Give me juice, I work with my heart."
*(**G**e = y, soft G. **J**uice = y. **J**ag = y. **J**obbar = y. **Hj**ärtat = y, H silent.)*

## Fun Fact: The Name "Johan"

The Swedish name Johan is pronounced /yoo-HAHN/ — with a Y at the start. Similarly:
- **Jonas** = /YOO-nas/
- **Johanna** = /yoo-HAN-ah/
- **Jan** = /yahn/
- **Jenny** = /YEN-ee/
- **Jessica** = /YES-i-kah/
- **Joakim** = /yoo-AH-kim/

If you meet a Swedish Johan and call him "Joe-han," he'll know you're not from around here.

## Summary

**What you learned:**
- J always sounds like "y" (as in "yes") — never like English J in "jam"
- DJ at the start of a word: D is silent, just say "y" (djur = yoor)
- LJ at the start of a word: L is silent, just say "y" (ljus = yoos)
- HJ at the start of a word: H is silent, just say "y" (hjälp = yelp)
- GJ at the start of a word: G is silent, just say "y" (gjort = yort)
- Soft G before E, I, Y, Ä, Ö also makes the "y" sound (from Lesson 4)
- Six different spellings, one sound — it's historical, not logical

**The one thing to remember:** J = Y. Always. Everything else in this lesson flows from that single fact.
