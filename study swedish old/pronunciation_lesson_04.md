# Pronunciation Lesson 4: Soft K and G — The Sounds That Change Everything

## What You'll Learn
This is the lesson that transforms how you read Swedish out loud. The letters K and G don't always sound like you'd expect — they completely change their pronunciation depending on which vowel follows them. Once you understand this rule, hundreds of Swedish words will suddenly make sense.

---

## The Rule That Changes Everything

Remember from Lesson 2 that Swedish vowels are split into two groups?

**Hard vowels:** A, O, U, Å
**Soft vowels:** E, I, Y, Ä, Ö

Here's the rule:

**K before a hard vowel = normal "k" sound (like English)**
**K before a soft vowel = "sh" sound (like "ship")**

**G before a hard vowel = normal "g" sound (like English "go")**
**G before a soft vowel = "y" sound (like English "yes")**

This is not optional or regional — it's a core rule of Swedish pronunciation that applies everywhere, every time. Let's break it down.

---

## K: Hard vs Soft

### Hard K (before A, O, U, Å) — Normal "k" sound

These work exactly like English. No surprises.

- **katt** /kaht/ — cat
- **kall** /kahl/ — cold
- **komma** /KOM-ah/ — to come
- **kosta** /KOS-tah/ — to cost
- **kung** /koong/ — king
- **kul** /kool/ — fun
- **kåpa** /KOH-pah/ — hood, cover

**Nothing new here.** K before hard vowels = the K you already know.

### Soft K (before E, I, Y, Ä, Ö) — "sh" sound

This is the one that shocks every beginner. The K is completely silent as a "k" — it becomes a "sh" or "ch" sound (technically the TJ-sound from Lesson 3, which is lighter than "sh" but very close).

- **kör** /shur/ — drives; choir
- **köpa** /SHUH-pah/ — to buy
- **kök** /shuhk/ — kitchen
- **känd** /shend/ — famous, known
- **källa** /SHEH-lah/ — source
- **kärlek** /SHAIR-lek/ — love
- **kel** /shehl/ — cuddle, caress
- **kind** /shind/ — cheek
- **kyla** /SHYH-lah/ — cold (noun), to chill
- **kyrka** /SHYR-kah/ — church

**Read that list again.** "Kök" (kitchen) is pronounced "shuhk." "Kyrka" (church) is "shyr-kah." "Kärlek" (love) is "shair-lek." If you've been saying hard K for these words, you've been saying them completely wrong — and now you know why.

---

## G: Hard vs Soft

### Hard G (before A, O, U, Å) — Normal "g" sound

Just like English "go." Straightforward.

- **gata** /GAH-tah/ — street
- **gammal** /GAH-mal/ — old
- **god** /good/ — good
- **gott** /got/ — tasty, good (ett-form)
- **gul** /gewl/ — yellow
- **gås** /gohs/ — goose

### Soft G (before E, I, Y, Ä, Ö) — "y" sound

The G becomes a "y" sound, like the beginning of English "yes."

- **göra** /YUH-rah/ — to do, to make
- **gärna** /YAIR-nah/ — gladly, happily
- **ge** /yeh/ — to give
- **gift** /yift/ — married; poison
- **gissa** /YIS-ah/ — to guess
- **gym** /yyhm/ — gym
- **gäst** /yest/ — guest
- **göra** /YUH-rah/ — to do
- **gömma** /YUM-ah/ — to hide

**So "göra" is not "goh-rah" — it's "yuh-rah."** And "gärna" is not "gair-nah" — it's "yair-nah." This changes how you read dozens of everyday words.

---

## The Complete Pattern

Here's the full picture in one view:

| Letter | Before Hard Vowel (A,O,U,Å) | Before Soft Vowel (E,I,Y,Ä,Ö) |
|--------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **K** | Normal "k" (like "cat") | "sh" sound (like "ship") |
| **G** | Normal "g" (like "go") | "y" sound (like "yes") |
| **SK** | Normal "sk" (like "skip") | SJ-sound (from Lesson 3) |

Notice SK is included here too — it follows the same pattern. You learned in Lesson 3 that SK before soft vowels makes the SJ-sound. Now you see it's part of this bigger rule.

## What About K and G at the End of Words?

At the end of a word, K and G usually keep their hard sounds regardless of what came before:

- **tack** /tahk/ — thanks (hard K at the end)
- **dag** /dahg/ — day (hard G at the end — though in casual speech the G often softens or disappears: "dah")

**The soft rule only applies when K or G come BEFORE a soft vowel** — not after one.

---

## The Silent G Rule (Bonus)

While we're talking about G, there's another essential rule:

**G at the end of -ig and -lig is usually silent.**

- **rolig** /ROH-lee/ — funny (NOT "roh-lig")
- **tråkig** /TROH-kee/ — boring (NOT "troh-kig")
- **viktig** /VIK-tee/ — important
- **vanlig** /VAN-lee/ — usual, normal
- **möjlig** /MOY-lee/ — possible
- **egentligen** /eh-YENT-lee-en/ — actually

This is one of the most common mistakes foreigners make. Every word ending in "-ig" drops the G sound. Once you know this, you'll immediately sound more natural.

**Exception:** When -ig is followed by a suffix that starts with a vowel, the G can come back. For example: "roliga" (funny, plural) is /ROH-lee-gah/ — the G returns because it's now before an A.

---

## GN and GJ: More G Combinations

A few more G patterns to know:

### GN at the start of a word — the G is silent
- **gnälla** /NEHL-ah/ — to whine
- **gnaga** /NAH-gah/ — to gnaw
- **gnista** /NIS-tah/ — spark

### GJ at the start — just a "y" sound
- **gjort** /yort/ — done (past participle of göra)
- **gjuta** /YOO-tah/ — to cast, to pour

## Practice Exercise

Read these sentences out loud. Pay attention to every K and G:

**1. Kan du köpa kaffe i köket?**
/kan doo SHUH-pah KAH-feh ee SHUH-ket/
"Can you buy coffee in the kitchen?"
*(First K is hard — "kan" — before A. Second K is soft — "köpa" — before Ö. Third K is hard — "kaffe" — before A. Fourth K is soft — "köket" — before Ö.)*

**2. Det är en gul och grön kyrka.**
/deh air en gewl ohk gruhn SHYR-kah/
"It's a yellow and green church."
*(G is hard in "gul" — before U. G is hard in "grön" — before R. K is soft in "kyrka" — before Y.)*

**3. Gärna! Ge mig en kopp kaffe.**
/YAIR-nah! yeh mey en kop KAH-feh/
"Gladly! Give me a cup of coffee."
*(G is soft in "gärna" — before Ä. G is soft in "ge" — before E. K is hard in "kopp" and "kaffe" — before O and A.)*

**4. Min kära gäst gillar att göra roliga saker.**
/min SHEH-rah yest YIL-ar aht YUH-rah ROH-lee-ah SAH-ker/
"My dear guest likes to do funny things."
*(K soft in "kära." G soft in "gäst," "gillar," "göra." Silent G in "roliga" — but it returns because of the -a suffix.)*

## Quick Reference Card

**K + A, O, U, Å → "k"** (normal)
**K + E, I, Y, Ä, Ö → "sh"** (soft)

**G + A, O, U, Å → "g"** (normal)
**G + E, I, Y, Ä, Ö → "y"** (soft)

**SK + A, O, U, Å → "sk"** (normal)
**SK + E, I, Y, Ä, Ö → SJ-sound** (from Lesson 3)

**-ig / -lig endings → G is silent**

## Summary

**What you learned:**
- K sounds like "sh" before soft vowels (E, I, Y, Ä, Ö) — this is the biggest surprise for beginners
- G sounds like "y" before soft vowels — "göra" is "yuh-rah," not "goh-rah"
- SK follows the same pattern — "sk" before hard vowels, SJ-sound before soft vowels
- K and G keep their hard sounds at the end of words
- G is silent at the end of -ig and -lig words ("rolig" = "roh-lee")
- GN at the start of a word = silent G
- GJ at the start = "y" sound

**Why this matters:** This single rule affects hundreds of the most common Swedish words. Understanding it is the difference between reading Swedish like a beginner and reading it like someone who actually knows how the language works.
